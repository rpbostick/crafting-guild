# Bike Weather: effort on comfort ratings, names for saved outfits, and heat in the suggestion (no screen yet)

The Today view-model (`src/suggest/`) is ready for a screen except for four things the screen would get wrong today: a comfort rating stores no effort, so a hard ride rated just right teaches the app the rider runs warm; a saved outfit has no name; heat is a heat-index note where an estimated WBGT with its uncertainty is wanted; and a few failure cases are worded wrongly. This task fixes them in the model and the view-model, so the screen task after it is layout only.

Read first: `README.md`, all of `src/model/`, `src/recommend/` (especially `learn.ts`, `need.ts`, `reasons.ts`, `defaults.ts`, and `heat.ts` with `docs/research/heat-estimate.md`), all of `src/suggest/`, `docs/research/clothing-weather.md` §3.3 to §3.7.

## What to build
1. **Effort on a rating.** `Effort` (easy, moderate, hard) moves to the model so the model does not import from `src/recommend/`. A `ride` comfort rating stores the effort of the outing it was given for; an `everyday` rating stores none. `rateOutfit` takes it from the outing. `learn.ts` reads each rating at its own effort; a rating with no effort recorded is read at moderate, as now. Test: three just-right ratings of hard rides at a band's midpoint leave the learned offset at zero (today they move it by about -1.5 °C), and the same for easy rides.
2. **A name on a saved outfit.** `Outfit` gains an optional `name`: trimmed, not empty when present, at most 60 characters. Wherever a saved outfit is shown by the view-model it has a `title`: its name, or its garments as today when it has none.
3. **A suggested name.** `suggestedOutfitName(...)` builds a name from the conditions the outfit was chosen for and the mode, in the app's plain voice ("Cool showery ride", "Cold dry walk"): temperature word from the need's band, then rain, then wind only when it is the notable thing. When a saved outfit already has that name (compared without regard to case), the next free number is appended ("Cool showery ride 2", then 3). The ready suggestion carries the suggested name for saving what it shows, with exactly the garment ids to save. Tests cover each temperature word's edges, the numbering (including a gap: with "X" and "X 3" saved the next is "X 2"), and that a user's own name is never changed.
4. **The stored document is version 2.** The step from version 1 changes no record (effort and name are both optional in what is read); a version 1 document still loads and is written back as version 2; an export from this version is refused by nothing in this code that reads version 2. `describeExport` is unchanged. Tests: a version 1 document with garments, an outfit and ratings loads unchanged; a version 2 document with an effort and a name round-trips through export and import, `replace` and `merge`.
4a. **What was carried.** A comfort rating may record the garments carried on the outing, each with whether it was put on: yes, no, or not said. `rateOutfit` takes them; the one-step rating (a comfort level and nothing else) stores the suggested carried garments as "not said", and a detailed rating says for each. The garments recorded as worn are whatever the person says they wore: a rated outfit is a record, so it is not held to the one-per-layer rule (a rain jacket put on over a gilet is a valid thing to have worn). Learning reads warmth from what was worn, counting a carried garment only when it was put on. A helper reports, per garment, how often it was carried and how often put on, for a later screen. Tests for each.
4b. **Loading never locks the wardrobe.** A stored outfit that breaks the one-per-layer rule (pairs that were valid under earlier layers: a T-shirt with a sweater, bib shorts with tights, light with warm gloves, a neck gaiter with a scarf) must not make the whole document refuse to load, as it does now. The rule is checked when an outfit is saved or changed, not when a document is read or imported; a loaded outfit that breaks it is kept and reported as needing attention (a field on the view-model's saved outfit, with a sentence naming the two garments). Replace the test that pins the refusal.
4c. **One pair of footwear.** An outfit holds at most one garment whose type is footwear, in the model's rule and in the recommender's wearability rules (today everyday shoes and winter boots sit on different layers and can be combined). Test both.
5. **A rating matches its saved outfit.** `suggestToday` hands out a rating for the garments worn, not those carried; a saved outfit that includes a carried garment is then not recognised as the outfit rated (`sameGarments` in `recommend.ts`) and never earns `provenHere`. Match a rating to a saved outfit on its worn garments, or carry the saved outfit's id on the rating; choose the smaller change and test it.
6. **Heat.** Replace the heat-index note with the estimate from `src/recommend/heat.ts`, following the wiring list in `docs/research/heat-estimate.md`: the worst hour of the outing, the WBGT as a range with its best value, and the basis (estimated or measured sun). Confirm what skywright's `RideHour` carries (time, cloud cover, dew point) and name the conversion to what `worstHeatOfOuting` takes.
   - **The warning is simple and strict.** One scale for everyone, whatever the effort: the strictest column of the published thresholds, judged on the high end of the range. When heat stress is possible the app says so as a heat advisory in plain words: respect your limits, carry water, ease off on climbs, with the stronger levels saying more. `heatWarning` keeps its effort parameter and the per-effort columns as a named option that nothing selects yet (one constant chooses the policy, with a comment that the finer policy and a "used to the heat" setting are the intended later step), so the advanced version is a change of policy, not a rewrite. Test that all three efforts get the same level today, and that switching the constant gives the per-effort levels.
   - The sentences (in `reasons.ts` or `words.ts`, one place): the advisory; the estimated number with its caveat stated every time it is shown, in the document's measured terms; what to do at each level. The ready suggestion exposes heat as its own field (level, sentences, numbers, basis), not only inside the notes list. The heat index stays available as a number. `HEAT_SHOWN_AS` goes away if nothing reads it. Tests at each level, at night, and with a measured solar value.
   - **From the review of `heat.ts`, fix here:** the day count is one day ahead in leap years after 2000 (inherited from the C source): correct it, note the departure from the source in the comment, add a sun-position test against the fixture's 2024 `midZenithDeg` values, and let the expected table move by what the correction moves it, saying by how much. A night hour has basis `estimated-sun` and so is judged 1 °C stricter than a measured daytime hour though nothing about the sun is unknown at night: judge it as known. Compute the cloud-ratio percentile table in the test file as the main table is, so the range-end constants are reproducible. In the document: say beside the 704 hours that 64 more were dropped by quality control and what kind they were; pin or drop the second rider-airspeed pair; add to the wiring list that the Argonne notice and acknowledgment sentence must ship in the app's licences (create a `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` at the repo root with it and the other ported sources). Single-letter names in `heat.ts` and its test become words.
7. **Failure cases** (`suggestion.ts`): every `WeatherError` that is not `network` or a listed HTTP status falls through to "an app update is needed" with no retry; match the refusal kinds positively and treat the rest as retryable, with tests for an `http` error with no status and an unknown kind.
8. **Small things in the files this touches:** `offsetMsOfTime` (`outing.ts`) is a second UTC-offset parser beside `LOCAL_TIME` in `src/ui/format.ts`: share one. `capitalised` and the rain-class words exist in both `words.ts` and `reasons.ts`: export one of each. The comment at `outing.ts` justifying the throw ("the forecast is fetched for today from midnight") holds for one provider only; give the reason that covers both.
9. **README**: the model and suggestion sections describe effort, names, the version 2 document and the heat estimate. In `docs/research/clothing-weather.md` §3.6, mark the questions now settled (regions replace the single torso region and `packable` is a property of the type; effort is asked per ride and stored; the comfort prompt is a single rating; gaps name the garment type; heat is shown as an estimate with its uncertainty) with one line each pointing at the code, without rewriting the report.
