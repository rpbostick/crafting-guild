## Requirements (Patrick, 2026-09-27; see `plans/bike-weather/product.md`)
- **Where the body is going** (Patrick, 2026-10-05, after the body analysis and the 3D research): the figures must read as people; structure may come from a 3D model (his idea: 3D for poses and transformations, then a 2D transform to match Open Peeps). Later versions of Bike Weather may show the avatar in several poses, so every body should have a base pose and take a set of chosen anatomical poses; the build library holds proper 3D bodies across the ranges and may offer a few pre-rendered slider variations instead of continuous sliders (undecided: the spike measures both). Further out: create or find an open-source Hero Forge for 3D models usable for machine-learning-driven animation. Tool choice: Anny (Apache-2.0, MakeHuman CC0 data) as the engine, Blender as an optional workbench; store bodies and poses in neutral formats (glTF, joint rotations by bone name).
- **Shoulders follow the chest** (Patrick, 2026-10-01): the shoulder anchors to the chest, so shoulder width comes mainly from chest breadth; thicker arms add a little. Garments follow the body's shoulder, so fix shoulders in the body, not in the garments.
- **The easing reference has no chest roll** (Patrick, 2026-10-07 20:41 MDT): the 3D easing pose keeps its turn and lean but its shoulders are level to the front view, as easing-2 draws them (about 3°); the 2D rig follows the drawing, not a rolled reference. Shoulders are the same width in every pose.
- **At the hips and thighs the library bodies are the reference, not easing-2** (Patrick, 2026-10-07 16:07 MDT, after the pelvis evidence): easing-2 draws narrow thighs in wide trousers and opens its gap between the legs 3 cm above the crotch, so the medium's overlay bounds at the near hip and far thigh follow the join, with the reason recorded.
- **Hip and thigh width is flesh around the bone** (Patrick, 2026-10-07 11:48 MDT, on the pelvis join): the thigh's outline is centred on its femur in every pose; a straight build's abdomen runs almost straight into the pelvis, and rounder builds make the curve with fat and muscle on the leg, hip and torso, moving the leg bones little. A pelvis-width slider could move them later.
- **Shoulders never overhang the chest** (Patrick, 2026-10-05, on the medium-derived builds): round and broad had very broad shoulders over a narrower body and lost roundness. Real shoulders are only broad when the chest is broad; bring the chest out and the shoulders in, keep the deltoid round. Second reference figure **easing-shoulders**: easing-2 transformed to a build's shoulder width and height (slight vertical adjustment for perspective), overlaid like the medium. Task `task-fw-40-shoulders-chest.md`.
- **Arms hang from the armpit** (Patrick, 2026-10-01): the shoulder's anchor is the armpit; a hanging arm meets the torso at the armpit with no gap. Shoulder options round 1 → C (slope + width from chest and arm), "and go farther": round 2 `fw-shoulder-armpit`. Round 2 (2026-10-01): Patrick picked **E**, rounder toward the source art; armpit stays at 0.735; shoulders slider → ±1.5 cm deltoid offset; the round builds' midsection spike is back (belly beside the far forearm) and must go. - An open-source (MIT) avatar tool in TypeScript for React Native/Expo, with a small Expo demo app, released separately from Bike Weather, which consumes it.
- Users make an avatar that looks like them: **body shapes** (builds: slim to broad, height, chest/waist/hips) in one standing pose now, more poses (e.g. riding) later.

- **Real layering** (Patrick, 2026-09-28): clothing layers like real clothing: undergarments, base, mid, insulation, shell, coats; hats, snowpants, gloves, etc. Several colour-coded garments worn at once, lower layers visible under upper ones (collars, cuffs, hems; an open/cutaway view), layers removable and addable in a tidy, correct order. Future animation: "get dressed", "get undressed", "remove/add a layer", garments exploding out and flying back like FreeCell's win screen. Design implications: zones with ordered layer ranks and several garments per zone; each layer fits over what is beneath it; a dressing-order rule (what must come off first); `renderFigure` output as body + one group per garment with stable ids and anchors, plus an exploded-layout function (target transforms per garment in layer order); the app animates, the engine supplies positions and order; hand-drawn garments use the same model.
Guesses (tell Patrick if one turns out wrong): renders are SVG strings (react-native-svg in the app, resvg for tests and contact sheets); the body is drawn in the Open Peeps line style (black outline, flat fills) so heads and body match.
