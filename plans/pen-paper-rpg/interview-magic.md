## Magic (round2-analysis section 6)

### 1. How many engines?
**Three engines** (answered 20:36 by prompt): Wizardry (book, reliable), Sorcery (innate, freeform), spirit magic (another will). Everything else is a list, a theme or a stored form inside them.

### 2. Priestly magic
Typed answer: "it's a sorcery branch, the abilities are learned that way". So priestly magic is a branch of Sorcery, and its abilities (Bless, Cure, Turn, Vision and the like) are learned through that branch. Not a prayed-for spell list, not spirit magic.

### 3. Nature magic
**A branch of Sorcery**: innate and elemental; the druid improvises within themes such as beasts, plants and storm.

### 4. Illusion
**A list in Wizardry and also a Sorcery theme** (shadow, glamour).

Orchestrator's reading of 2 to 4 together: Sorcery is the home of the themed casters (priest, druid, illusionist-by-birth, elementalist), each a branch with its own abilities; Wizardry is the book list. To confirm when the Sorcery questions are asked.

### 5. Sorcery's limit
**What a branch is:** a theme plus learned abilities. Each branch (holy, nature, shadow, fire, chaos...) gives freeform casting inside its theme using the uniform table, plus a few named abilities learned with levels (Turn and Cure for holy, Shapeshift for nature). A sorcerer has one or two branches.

**Limits chosen (20:38):** a roll every casting; few branches; hit points. Typed addition: "they have some way of augmenting their abilities. maybe they spend the hit dice that would be the damage, but on augments?" Lasting corruption was not picked.

Orchestrator's reading of the addition (to confirm): a casting has a number of dice; each die is either rolled as damage or spent on an augment (range, area, duration, a second effect), so a bigger or longer spell hits less hard. Close to the d200 list's one currency.

**Augments (20:41): both.** Casting dice are split between damage and augments (each die is rolled for damage or strength, or spent on one step of the table), and the sorcerer can burn their own Hit Dice to add more dice when it counts. Hit points buy extra dice.

### 6. The Warp
**Becomes the chaos branch.** Chaos is one Sorcery branch and the Warp is its miss table. The Power score and its arithmetic go. The Muscle Wizard becomes a chaos sorcerer who fights.

### 7. Wizardry's limit
Typed answer: "finding and learning, but some sort of cost to limit the number cast". So no cap on spells held; spells are found, taught or researched; and a per-casting or per-day cost limits how many are cast. The form of that cost is the follow-up.

**The cost (20:41), typed:** "the spell book has a fatigue counter on it, like quantum gear. after they are used up, only rote spells may be cast. willing to workshop this, but basically the idea is that they can keep casting til they fail. maybe? i dunno, we need a conversation probably." To workshop: a fatigue counter on the book, marked by failed or strained castings, with rote spells always available once it is used up.

**Book fatigue (20:44): marked on a strained roll.** Every real spell is a roll, 2d6+INT. 10+: works, no mark. 7-9: works, mark one fatigue. 6 or less: fails, mark one. Each augment step marks one more on a 7-9 or less. Boxes full: rotes only until rest. Note typed: "the fatigue gets stronger over time. only 2 slots at lvl 1." Orchestrator's reading: the book has 2 fatigue boxes at level 1 and the number grows with level; confirm whether "stronger over time" means more boxes with level or a worsening penalty as boxes fill.

**Rotes, typed:** "a few chosen spells at the base level only, if cost is available, can cast with cost". Reading: a wizard chooses a few rote spells; each is always castable in its base form at no cost; while fatigue boxes remain, a rote can also be cast augmented at the usual cost.

**Fatigue, clarified (20:55): more boxes with level.** Two fatigue boxes at level 1, one more every level or two. No growing penalty.

### 8. Spirit magic

Typed (20:41): "look at WoDu - it looks like you can bind some directly, you can bind them to components... so if a slot based inventory is used, that might be something. but no double binding of spirits. probably one per level, ish, but let's workshop it for balance". So: a spirit can be bound directly or into a component or object; an object-bound spirit could take an inventory slot if the game uses slots; one spirit cannot be bound twice; about one per level, to be balanced.

Sources checked: the 1979 game prices each summoning (an hour, a dose of quicksilver, hit points, a circle; quicksilver doses a day limited by level). Remix needs a token of a suitable environment to call a spirit and keeps it for one task per point spent.

**Binding (20:44): in vessels.** A spirit is bound into a vessel (ring, flask, staff, bone) that takes an inventory slot and can be lost, stolen or broken, or into the summoner's own body (no slot, cannot be stolen, its will presses on you). One spirit, one binding. Calling a bound spirit is fast; calling an unbound one costs the 1979 price. Note typed: "they can bind two + 1/2 per HD, there are also special magic items that can bind them". So the cap is 2 + half the summoner's Hit Dice (rounding to settle), and special magic items can hold a bound spirit beyond or apart from that cap.

### 9. The glass cannon

Typed (20:41): "probably sorcerer, but a lot of the 'sorcerer' magic classes are not glass cannons. maybe anyone who can pay." So: any caster who pays their engine's price can reach the top damage; the sorcerer paying hit points is the likeliest, but holy, nature and illusion sorcerers are not cannons by default. The multiplier is still open.

**Top damage (20:44): x1.5 sustained, x2 as a burst.** A paying caster keeps up about 1.5 times the median for a fight; a once-a-fight burst (burning Hit Dice, a great spirit) can reach about 2 times.

### 10. Song magic and the Skald

**A Sorcery branch (20:47).** Song or voice is a branch like holy and nature: freeform within its theme plus learned abilities such as Pacify. The Skald is its caster; the Bard stays a performer without spells. Compose goes.

### 11. Alchemy and Gadgetry

Typed (20:47): "i need a bigger explanation here. show me all the classes and explain how they currently work, then give proposed detailed checklist for them. allow me to check more than one box." Explanation and a multi-select checklist to follow.

**Checklist answers (20:52), after a fuller explanation in chat.**

Maker rules, all four ticked:
- One maker's rule: every made thing (potion, scroll, balm, device, magic item) is an effect from the uniform table, paid in advance in time and silver, times its uses.
- Formulas are learned like spells: start with three; more are found, taught or researched; no cap.
- Devices use locked capacity: at most 2 + half Hit Dice working devices, the same cap as bound spirits. Consumables are limited only by silver and time.
- Devices need fuel for every use (blood, quicksilver, holy water, a debility).

Abilities, all four ticked:
- Alchemy is the ability for consumables (potions, balms, scrolls, poisons).
- Gadgetry is AWoD's, narrowed: +INT only when you name a gadget you prepared (a few per day); spell devices come by the maker's rule, not at fixed levels.
- Remix's device-at-level-up is dropped.
- Engineer folds into Gadgetry (machines, clockwork, firearms).
- Typed: "there might be multiple tinker abilities for different classes of things, so any two engineers may not all have the same abilities". Reading: Gadgetry or Tinker may come as several abilities by kind of thing (for example locks and traps, clockwork, firearms, magical devices), so two engineers can differ.

Maker classes: "Alchemist joins the main list", "Magitek Engineer joins the main list" and "Both stay optional" were all ticked, which conflict. To clarify.

Who else:
- Assassin keeps Alchemy as a choice.
- Druid loses formulas (nature is a Sorcery branch).
- Any wizard can scribe a known spell as a scroll with the maker's rule, without Alchemy.
- A summoner makes spirit vessels with the maker's rule.

**Maker classes, clarified (20:55): both join the main list.** The Alchemist and the Magitek Engineer are main classes.

## Trimming (round2-analysis section 7)

### 1. Pairing-only classes

**Remove all of them (20:47):** Ironguard, Burglar, Warden, Forge Priest, Beastmaster, Adventurer, Cavalier, and the old Sorcerer and Summoner as written (the Sorcery and spirit classes are rebuilt under the three engines). Each can still be built as a custom class.

### 2. Single-class skills

**Back to the nine (20:47).** Focus, Music and Mechanics become part of the Monk's, Bard's and Engineer's abilities; Mysteries and Culture fold into Lore, Tactics into Leadership, Medicine into Heal; Use Devices goes; Craft folds into the maker abilities.

### 3. Tumble, Opportunist and Gambit, Remix's Expert

**All four go (20:55):** Tumble is removed; Gambit is merged into one stronger Opportunist; Remix's Expert is removed; AWoD's Expert is removed (the Stranger and the Alchemist get Lucky instead).

### 4. Raw Chaos magic and the Muscle Wizard

Answered under magic question 6: Chaos becomes a Sorcery branch with the Warp as its miss table; the Power score goes; the Muscle Wizard becomes a chaos sorcerer who fights.

### 5. Hit points

**As written in the round 2 note (20:55):** 4 HP + CON per level to level 6, then + CON per level. Hit Dice are for recovery only (and, for sorcerers, for burning on spells). A quick break after a fight restores 4 + CON.
